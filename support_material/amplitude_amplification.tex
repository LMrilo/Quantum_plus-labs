It assumes the bra--ket notation of the labs: the inner product $\braket{a}{b}$ of two states, and the outer product
$\ket{w}\bra{w}$, which acts on a state as $\ket{w}\bra{w}\,\ket{\psi} = \braket{w}{\psi}\,\ket{w}$.
It also uses sines and cosines of an angle and the idea of reflecting a vector across a line in a plane.

\section*{The Geometric Picture}

Grover's algorithm operates in a two-dimensional subspace of the full $N$-dimensional Hilbert space ($N=2^n$ for $n$ qubits). The key insight is that the algorithm's state vector always remains in the plane that contains two vectors:

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\ket{s}$: the uniform superposition
    \[
    \ket{s} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{x=0}^{N-1}\ket{x},\qquad N=2^n.
    \]
  \item $\ket{w}$: the target (marked) state.
\end{itemize}

These two vectors are \emph{not} orthogonal: $\braket{w}{s} = 1/\sqrt{N}$.
Define instead the normalized sum of all the other basis states,
\[
\ket{s'} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N-1}}\sum_{x\neq w}\ket{x},
\]
which is orthogonal to $\ket{w}$. The pair $\{\ket{w},\ket{s'}\}$ is an orthonormal basis of the plane (not of the full space, which has $N$ dimensions). All the calculations below use this basis.

\section*{Initial State and Angle $\theta$}

The algorithm starts in $\ket{s}$. We can write
\[
\ket{s} = \sin\theta\ket{w} + \cos\theta\ket{s'},
\]
where
\[
\sin\theta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}},\qquad
\cos\theta = \sqrt{\frac{N-1}{N}},\qquad
\theta = \arcsin\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right).
\]
So $\theta$ is the angle between $\ket{s}$ and the $\ket{s'}$ axis.
For large $N$, $\theta\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}$ is small. For $N=8$, $\theta\approx 0.3614$~rad $\approx 20.7^\circ$.

\section*{Grover Iteration as Reflections}

A single Grover iteration $G = DO$ consists of two reflections:

\subsection*{1. Oracle Reflection $O$}

The oracle reflects about the hyperplane orthogonal to $\ket{w}$:
\[
O = I - 2\ket{w}\bra{w}.
\]
It flips the sign of the $\ket{w}$ component and leaves every state orthogonal to $\ket{w}$ unchanged.
Geometrically, $O$ is a reflection across the line $\ket{s'}$ in our plane.

\subsection*{2. Diffusion Reflection $D$}

The diffusion operator reflects about $\ket{s}$:
\[
D = 2\ket{s}\bra{s} - I.
\]

\subsection*{Composition: Rotation by $2\theta$}

The product of two reflections in a plane is a rotation by twice the angle between their axes. Here the two axes are $\ket{s'}$ (for $O$) and $\ket{s}$ (for $D$), at angle $\theta$, so $DO$ rotates the state vector by angle $2\theta$ toward $\ket{w}$:
\[
\ket{s} \xrightarrow{G} \ket{\psi_1} \xrightarrow{G} \ket{\psi_2} \xrightarrow{G} \cdots \xrightarrow{G} \ket{\psi_R} \approx \ket{w}.
\]

After $R\approx\frac{\pi}{4}\sqrt{N}$ iterations, the state is nearly $\ket{w}$.

\section*{Mathematical Derivation}

\subsection*{State After One Iteration}

Start with $\ket{s} = \sin\theta\ket{w} + \cos\theta\ket{s'}$.

\textbf{Oracle reflection:}
\[
O\ket{s}
= \sin\theta\,O\ket{w} + \cos\theta\,O\ket{s'}
= -\sin\theta\ket{w} + \cos\theta\ket{s'},
\]
which is $\ket{s}$ reflected across the $\ket{s'}$ axis: it makes angle $-\theta$ with $\ket{s'}$, and angle $\frac{\pi}{2}+\theta$ with $\ket{w}$ (its overlap with $\ket{w}$ is $-\sin\theta = \cos(\frac{\pi}{2}+\theta)$).

\textbf{Diffusion reflection:}
Since $D$ reflects about $\ket{s}$,
\[
D\ket{\psi^-} = 2\braket{s}{\psi^-}\,\ket{s} - \ket{\psi^-},
\]
where $\ket{\psi^-} = O\ket{s}$. Compute
\[
\braket{s}{\psi^-} = \sin\theta(-\sin\theta) + \cos\theta(\cos\theta)
             = \cos(2\theta).
\]
Thus
\[
D\ket{\psi^-}
= 2\cos(2\theta)\ket{s} - \ket{\psi^-}.
\]

In the $\{\ket{w},\ket{s'}\}$ basis, the $\ket{w}$ component is $2\cos(2\theta)\sin\theta + \sin\theta = \sin(3\theta)$ and the $\ket{s'}$ component is $2\cos(2\theta)\cos\theta - \cos\theta = \cos(3\theta)$ (using $2\cos2\theta\,\sin\theta = \sin3\theta - \sin\theta$ and $2\cos2\theta\,\cos\theta = \cos3\theta + \cos\theta$). So
\[
\ket{\psi_1} = \sin(3\theta)\ket{w} + \cos(3\theta)\ket{s'}.
\]
The angle with $\ket{s'}$ increased from $\theta$ to $3\theta$---a rotation by $2\theta$.

\subsection*{General Iteration}

After $k$ iterations,
\[
\ket{\psi_k} = \sin\bigl((2k+1)\theta\bigr)\ket{w}
             + \cos\bigl((2k+1)\theta\bigr)\ket{s'}.
\]
The probability of measuring $\ket{w}$ is
\[
P_k = \sin^2\bigl((2k+1)\theta\bigr).
\]
$P_k$ reaches its first peak when the angle $(2k+1)\theta$ is closest to $\frac{\pi}{2}$, that is for $k$ close to $\frac{\pi}{4\theta}-\frac12$. The standard choice is
\[
k_{\text{opt}} = \left\lfloor\frac{\pi}{4\theta}\right\rfloor \approx \frac{\pi}{4}\sqrt{N},
\]
for which $(2k_{\text{opt}}+1)\theta$ is within $\theta$ of $\frac{\pi}{2}$, so $P_{k_{\text{opt}}} \ge \cos^2\theta = 1-\frac{1}{N}$.
For $N=8$: $\frac{\pi}{4\theta}\approx 2.17$, $k_{\text{opt}}=2$ and $P_2=\sin^2(5\theta)\approx 0.945$.
More iterations do not help: the rotation continues past $\ket{w}$, and $P_3=\sin^2(7\theta)\approx 0.330$.

\section*{Amplitude Amplification Generalization}

The same geometric picture underlies \emph{amplitude amplification}, a generalization where the initial state $\ket{s}$ has a small overlap with a ``good'' subspace $\mathcal{G}$ spanned by several basis states. Let $\Pi_{\mathcal{G}} = \sum_{x\in\mathcal{G}}\ket{x}\bra{x}$ be the projector onto $\mathcal{G}$, and $\sin\theta$ the length of $\Pi_{\mathcal{G}}\ket{s}$. The oracle becomes $O = I - 2\Pi_{\mathcal{G}}$ (it flips the sign of every good component), and $D = 2\ket{s}\bra{s}-I$ still reflects about the prepared state. The picture above then holds with $\ket{w}$ replaced by the normalized good part $\Pi_{\mathcal{G}}\ket{s}/\sin\theta$: each iteration rotates by $2\theta$, and the projection onto $\mathcal{G}$ grows until its first peak.

This framework extends Grover's algorithm to quantum counting, collision finding, and many optimization problems.

\section*{Key Insights}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Grover's algorithm is a \emph{rotation} in a 2D subspace by fixed angle $2\theta$ per iteration.
  \item The oracle $O$ ``marks'' the target by phase flip (reflection).
  \item The diffusion $D$ amplifies via inversion about the mean (second reflection).
  \item Optimal iterations $\sim\frac{\pi}{4}\sqrt{N}$ yield success probability at least $1-\frac{1}{N}$; stopping later lowers it again.
  \item Quadratic speedup in the number of oracle queries for unstructured search: $O(\sqrt{N})$ vs.\ classical $O(N)$.
\end{itemize}
